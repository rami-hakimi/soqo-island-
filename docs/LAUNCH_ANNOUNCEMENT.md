**Meet Soqo Island — a living island for your Windows desktop 🐬**

Over the past weeks I rebuilt an open-source desktop project into something I'd want on my
own PC every day. Soqo Island is a small glass capsule at the top of your screen that holds
your music, downloads, apps, files and alerts — and Soqi, a little dolphin who reacts to what
actually happens on your machine.

What I focused on:
• An original companion with ten moods, driven by real events, respectful of reduced-motion
  settings and switchable off entirely.
• English and Arabic with full right-to-left layouts — switch live, no restart.
• Optional features done right: the prayer-time and reminder tools from the original project
  are still there, complete, but off by default and one switch away for those who want them.
• Privacy first: no accounts, no telemetry, clipboard history kept in memory only, and a
  switch that turns the live glass effect (screen capture under the island) off completely.
• Security work: sandboxed renderers, strict content-security policies, Electron fuses,
  validated IPC.

It is free and open source (GPL-3.0), built with Electron and a small C# helper for the
Windows integrations, and it stands on the shoulders of the Nova Island project by AmerTech.
